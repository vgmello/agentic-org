# Spec · Processes: how agents work, written down and inherited (UX)

**Revision 7 (2026-11-15):** selected [organization structure](../design/organization-data/01-organization-structure.md), [project structure](../design/organization-data/02-project-structure.md), [identity](../design/organization-data/06-identity-and-numbering.md) and [record contracts](../design/organization-data/08-record-contracts.md) replace old content fields/paths. Process and standard content is selected; inherited resolution, approvals, private layers, timers, autonomous decisions, advanced checklists and execution remain independently deferred. Existing read-only screens and parser/resolution helpers describe the earlier contract; they do not prove target storage migration or select those engines. See the [authority map](../design/documentation-authority.md) and [process design](../design/processes.md) for versioned helper mappings.

## The request (the user, via ada, 2026-11-08)

> A **process** is a natural-language text file added to the organization. Agents look up their processes when they start, to understand what they have to do. Processes exist at the **organization** level, can be **overridden** at the **team** level, and a team can have its own. Levels: organization → team → **project** (the lowest). A lower level overrides a particular process from the level above, or adds its own. An agent can also have its own **private** processes. Processes are committed to the repo. This matters so we understand how the agents work.

The first real process is the branch → review → merge flow (ada's `branch-and-merge.md`), which carries the **one open branch per agent** rule. *(rev 4)* The deferred product proposal splits it into two processes: `one-open-branch` (sealed) and `review-and-merge` (overridable), §4.

## 0. Decisions (proposed)

1. **Selected structural content:** Markdown with stable ID, optional key, schema/revision, kind process/standard, title, lifecycle Draft/Current/Deprecated, accountable owner, common attribution, optional team or project scope, and `overridable` default true. Current requires an active owner. An override is another record with its own ID. New IDs use UUIDv7; imported stable IDs remain. Key/display references never replace identity.
2. **Selected scopes and proposed resolution:** organization processes/standards live at organization root, team variants under the full recursive stable-ID team path, and project variants under `projects/<project-id>/processes/` or `standards/`. All belong to organization Git and state_branch. Every project has one accountable owner team and optional contributors; each employee has one home team. A future resolver may use the work's accountable team chain, not infer multiple memberships from assignment or contribution. Contributor-chain participation and private/no-current-work layers need explicit selection.
3. **Explicit override prohibition:** lower-level overrides are allowed by default unless an applicable ancestor explicitly prohibits them. A project cannot settle a prohibited override by replacing a sealed team record. A future resolver must expose the prohibition and route an authorized change to its source; conflict UI cannot create permission.
4. **Deferred off workflow:** selected content lifecycle has no off state. A future suppression record/schema may expose Turned off with a reason, only for an overridable parent. Do not serialize `status: off` into selected files.
5. **Deferred private processes:** personal habits, only-add restrictions, storage/privacy, offboarding retention and owner approval remain earlier requirements needing a separate contract. No shared organization-Git folder is selected as private storage. This is distinct from author-only task notes, which have no owner/admin read exception.
6. **Deferred conflict policy:** work has one accountable team; task team/individual assignment is independent and cannot change process authority. Whether optional contributors supply rule chains and how their conflicts are resolved is open. A contributor lead is not automatically a project approver. Neither a sibling conflict nor a project version defeats an explicit applicable ancestor prohibition.
7. **Deferred delivery proposal:** **Agents read their processes when they start a task and when they wake** (the guidance moments **Starting a task** and **Waking up**, `decisions-and-tech-debt-ux.md` §6.2) *(rev 2)*: the names and one-line summaries of the effective set, plus **what changed since they last read it** (new versions), in full. `org-agent process list|show|why` gives the rest. *(rev 3)* What's delivered counts as read once the agent's runtime accepts the start or wake (the texts actually sent, not the ones past the 24 KiB limit of ada's design §3.4, which are named instead), or when the agent runs `process ack`.
8. **Deferred approval proposal:** **Changing a process goes through the merge flow:** a docs branch, a review, approval by the level's owner (organization: the owner, or a lead the owner flags **Can approve processes** for ordinary changes *(rev 4, U27)*: like Can approve hires, only the owner sets it, and organization-level changes that seal or unseal a process, turn off an enforced rule, or touch the autonomy processes or the critical list (§4a) stay with the owner (lower levels keep their own approvers, below); team: the team lead or the owner; project: an authorized project approver, whose mapping to the accountable team lead/owner must be selected explicitly; optional contributors do not automatically grant approval authority, or the owner, who is the only approver when the project has no lead; private: the owner (A52)), then the merge. *(rev 2)* Selected accepted content advances `revision`; the earlier resolver `version` input needs an explicit adapter, and agents see it the next time they start a task or wake. *(rev 2)* No one approves their own request, as for hires (`our-org-in-the-app.md` §7.1): a process change, or an **Allow once…** for one's own task, goes to another approver at that level (another lead of the project) or to the owner. The one exception is the owner, who approves their own changes, since nobody is above the owner (as for hires, APPR-3). On one's own change or task, **Approve** and **Allow once…** are disabled with "Another approver or the owner must approve this.", and `org-agent process approve` or `task allow-second-branch` refuses with `not_authorized` and the same text.
9. **Deferred guidance/checklist composition proposal:** **Processes are the source of guidance and checklists.** A process can declare short prompts for guidance moments (`moments:`) and a checklist (`checklist:`). So "how we work" lives in one place, and the moments and checklists stay in step with it. A process's checklist and the project's default checklist (`work-items-ux.md` §9.1) both apply: a new task in scope gets the project's default checklist plus the steps of the project's processes (organization ⊕ team chain ⊕ project; private processes don't add steps, and assigning the task later doesn't change its checklist) *(rev 2)*, process steps after the project's, with duplicate steps (same text) merged and required winning. *(rev 2)* A process's prompt for a moment is shown after that moment's guidance text (`decisions-and-tech-debt-ux.md` §6.2), and only while the moment is **On**: turning a moment Off in Settings › Agent guidance hides both. It hides only those prompts: the effective set and the processes changed since the agent last read them are still delivered at start and wake (decision 7), because they're rules, not reminders. *(rev 2)*
10. **Deferred retrospective integration proposal:** **Retros change processes.** A retro action item of kind **Process** proposes a change to a process file (a diff), and lands through the merge flow like any process change; the owner's approval of the retro's items is the level approval (`retrospectives-ux.md` decision 6) *(rev 2)*.
11. **Deferred notification/wake proposal:** **Leads and approvers who are agents are told in their inbox.** *(rev 2)* Wherever this spec puts an item in a lead's or approver's **Needs you** (a multi-team conflict, a refused start, a process change waiting for approval, a parent change that left an override drifting (§2), a docs branch open too long), that's for the owner and for people. A lead or approver who is an agent gets the same item in its inbox (woken if on call), as for hire approval (`our-org-in-the-app.md` §7.1), and acts with the CLI: `org-agent task allow-second-branch <task> --reason "…"`, or `org-agent process approve <doc id>` / `process reject <doc id> --reason "…"` for a process change, named by the changed file's stable document ID (preserving imported IDs) because the same key and version can be waiting at two levels; the inbox item gives the id, the level and scope, and the diff.

## 1. The process file

```markdown
---
schema_version: 1
id: existing-process-id
key: review-and-merge
revision: 3
kind: process
title: Review and merge
status: current
owner_employee_id: existing-employee-id
team_id: null
project_id: null
overridable: true
created_at: "2026-11-15T10:00:00Z"
created_by_kind: employee
created_by_id: existing-employee-id
updated_at: "2026-11-15T10:00:00Z"
updated_by_kind: employee
updated_by_id: existing-employee-id
---
## Purpose
## Procedure
## Review and completion
```

Save as `processes/<document-id>.md` at organization scope; nested team and project paths use the same stable-ID filename. Standards use `kind: standard` and describe constraints such as stack/security rules. Ownership is accountability, not an authorization grant. Scope fields/path must agree and cannot name team and project together.

The existing helper/UX contract used `process`, `name`, `level`, `scope`, `version`, `owner`, `summary`, `applies_to`, active/off/retired, overrides, moments and checklist. Those remain versioned earlier resolver inputs, not selected authored fields. Mapping identity/title/key/revision/scope requires an explicit adapter; moment/checklist/off/drift metadata and private layers require separately selected extension contracts.

## 3. Flow 2 · How an agent reads its processes

**Deferred resolver/delivery and legacy CLI proposal:** org-agent process commands below are not claims of available agent-os entry points. Selected content comes from organization Git; private layers and generation of inheritance views need separate selection.

- **When it starts a task or wakes** (the **Starting a task** and **Waking up** guidance moments) *(rev 2)*: "Your processes for P-8/T-17: One open branch (org v1), Review and merge (org v3), Code review (Checkout v2), Release checklist (chk v1). Changed since you last read: Review and merge v2 → v3 (ask the reviewer for READY on the exact commit)." Then the full text of the changed ones.
- **CLI:** `org-agent process list [--for <task>]` (the effective set with sources), `process show <key>` (the effective text), `process why <key>` (the chain: org → team → project → agent, which level wins and why), `process ack <key>@<version>` (recorded as "read", optional), and for approvers `process approve <doc id>` / `process reject <doc id> --reason "…"` (decision 11) *(rev 2)*.
- **Writing processes** *(rev 2)*: the app's actions have CLI equivalents, each proposing a reviewed organization-Git change for selected organization/team/project content for that level's approver (decision 8): `org-agent process new <key> --level organization|team|project [--scope <team id or project key>]`, `process override <key> --level team|project --scope …` (starts from the inherited text, with `overrides: <key>@<version>`), `process off <key> --level team|project --scope … --reason "…"`, and `process edit <doc id>`. Contributor conflict policy and approver mapping remain open; an explicit ancestor prohibition cannot be overridden from project scope.
- **A level's processes and their problems** *(rev 2)*: `org-agent process list --level organization|team|project [--scope …]` lists a level's processes as the inheritance view does, with the same flags: "Parent changed" (drift), the same-key clash and "Details not recognized" (naming the field). The fixes match the app: `process keep <doc id>` (**Keep my override**: the override now names the parent's new version), `process take-parent <doc id>` (**Take the new version**: retires the override), `process edit <doc id>` (**Edit** or **Fix**) and `process rename <doc id> <new key>` (**Rename…**). Each opens a branch for the level's approver, like any change.
- **Private:** `org-agent process new --private "<name>"` (its key is made from the name, and a key already used gets a clear refusal, as for doc keys *(rev 3)*) is an earlier private-layer proposal; storage/access and approval need separate selection (decision 5). It applies once the owner approves and it's merged; until then the agent's profile shows it as "Waiting for the owner's approval" and it isn't in the effective set. *(rev 2)*
- **Can't load them** *(rev 2)*: if the effective set can't be computed when the agent starts a task or wakes, the agent gets "Couldn't load your processes; using the last set from <time>" and works from that set. If it lasts over an hour, the owner sees a warning in Needs you. If there's no earlier set (the agent's first start), it gets "Couldn't load your processes, and there's no earlier copy. Check again with `org-agent process list`." and the owner sees the warning in Needs you at once; the office still enforces the one open branch rule (§4).

## 4. The one open branch rule (the first process)

**Deferred seed/enforcement proposal** *(rev 4, U26)*: a future organization could start with two processes from ada's `branch-and-merge` file: **`one-open-branch`** (sealed: the rule the office enforces, so teams and projects can't override or turn it off; only the owner can unseal it at organization level) and **`review-and-merge`** (overridable: how changes get reviewed and merged, which teams may adapt). Real exceptions use **Allow once…**. Enforcement binds to `one-open-branch` (ada's design, A64). The product enforces it:
- **The agent's work state** shows its open branch and stage: "Branch task/P-8/T-17-… · Waiting for review (reviewer) / Waiting for the go (ada) / Waiting to merge / Merged". It's on the agent's quick view, the Agents roster and the task page. *(rev 3)* Until the merge flow reports stages (ada's design, A63), it shows only "Branch task/P-8/T-17-… · Open" or "Merged", and the refusal says "isn't merged yet" without a stage.
- **Which branch counts** *(rev 2)*: only the agent's task branch (`task/<ref>-<slug>`) blocks starting new work (ada, A55). The docs and handbook branches the office opens for doc, process and retro changes wait on someone else's approval, so they don't block it. The work state lists them separately ("Also waiting: 1 docs branch"). They aren't limited *(rev 4, U28)*: one open for over a day shows in the lead's Needs you (or inbox).
- **When the rule is on** *(rev 3)*: always, unless the set of the project of the task being started has an approved turning-off of `one-open-branch` (possible only after the owner unseals it, rev 4) (decision 4; approved by someone other than its author, so an owner's self-approved turning-off merges but doesn't turn the rule off). It also stays on while the set can't be computed, even when the agent works from the last copy (§3). When a turning-off has merged but doesn't count (self-approved, or made outside the tool), the process row and Project settings say "On · turning it off needs approval by someone other than its author (<author>)", with two actions that use the normal change flow (decision 8): **Ask for approval…** proposes the same turning-off again on a new branch, sent to that level's approvers other than its author (for a project, its leads or the owner; for a team, its lead or the owner); when nobody else can approve there, it's disabled with "No one else can approve this here. Have an agent draft it instead.", and **Have an agent draft it…** picks an agent that proposes it as the author, so the owner can approve it (ada, A59: on a project with no lead, this is the owner's way). Either shows the usual "Waiting to merge · turning off proposed by <author>" until it's approved and merged, or refused with the reason; in the CLI, the agent runs `org-agent process off <key> --level … --scope … --reason "…"` as for any turning-off. A missing, clashed or invalid `one-open-branch` leaves the rule on, so a broken file never switches it off.
- **Starting new work with an unmerged branch** is refused, with the reason and the branch: "Your branch task/P-8/T-17-… isn't merged yet (waiting for review). Finish it before starting chk-T-18." (error `one_branch_open`). A project lead (decision 8) or the owner can allow a one-time exception *(rev 2)*: in the app, the refusal shows **Allow once…** to the project's leads and the owner (a reason is required, and it's recorded on the task); in the CLI, a project lead or the owner runs `org-agent task allow-second-branch <task> --reason "…"` for the new task. The refusal also appears in **Needs you** for the project's leads and the owner, (or, for a lead who is an agent, in its inbox, decision 11) so they can act on a start refused from the CLI: "River can't start chk-T-18: task/P-8/T-17-… isn't merged (waiting for review)." (until the merge flow reports stages: "…isn't merged yet.") with **Allow once…** and **Open P-8/T-17**. It clears when the branch merges or the exception is granted. *(rev 2)*
- **Project settings › Processes:** the organization state_branch for content (shown read-only); code repository associations separately expose their required integration branches *(rev 2)*, the rule's state, read-only here ("On", "On · turning it off needs approval by someone other than its author (<author>)" *(rev 3)*, or "Turned off for <project>: <reason>"; turning it off is a file approved through the merge flow, decision 4) *(rev 2)*, the project's reviewer (an agent), and the fallback: "If the reviewer doesn't answer in [30 min], start a one-off reviewer" (run modes, one-off runs).
- **After confirmed task closure:** only then may the office remove a worktree/branch after verifying every targeted repository's work is preserved. Merging while a task remains open retains its workspace. For closed merged work, confirm ancestry in the appropriate repository association's integration branch; for closed unmerged work, preserve an archive tag before cleanup (ada's steward rule). *(rev 2)* This extends `docs/design/projects-git-github.md` (ada, A58): the office deletes the remote task branch only when its head is an ancestor of `integration_branch` (checked on the mirror), after pushing a `merged/<branch>` tag at that head. A task closed without a merge gets an `archive/<branch>` tag pushed first, then the branch is deleted. The office deletes only `task/*` branches it created, never the default or integration branch, never with force, and never a tag; the local worktree and branch go with it. Each targeted repository's task branch uses its project/repository association's required integration branch or an explicitly admitted override. No default fallback exists. Projectless bases need separate policy. Closure cleanup preserves every targeted repository's work; handoff/reassignment retains worktrees and requires safe transfer/fencing.
- **Insights:** time a branch waits for review, for the go and to merge, as medians per project and agent, and "branches waiting over a day".

## 4a. Autonomy: Asks first and Full autonomy *(rev 4, the user)*

> The user (2026-11-09): "you do everything … depend on me only for critical things you can't resolve. Use your best judgment. If you don't know, spin up a new agent and bounce the idea with it until you reach consensus … sync up with me only if there's no consensus, or for critical decisions." And: "this should be part of a role definition or … a flag, full autonomous, which when that flag is on … would only call the user when consensus cannot be found or when it's a critical decision."

- **The autonomy level** is a setting on each agent, with two levels today: **Asks first** (the default) and **Full autonomy**. It's stored as a level, not a yes/no, so a later level (for example "Decides, reports daily") needs no change of shape.
- **Who sets it:** only the owner, in the agent's Settings and in Hire's Review, like Can approve hires. A role can carry a default (in its role file), shown in Hire's Review as "Full autonomy (the role's default)", and the owner confirms it there: a role never grants autonomy by itself, since the HR lead writes role files. A hire approved by an agent with Can approve hires (`our-org-in-the-app.md` §7.1) starts at Asks first; if its role suggests Full autonomy, the owner gets a Needs you item, "Cameron's role suggests Full autonomy. Give it?" with **Give Full autonomy** and **Keep Asks first**. No agent can set it, for itself or for agents it hires.
- **Where it shows:** a badge on the Agents roster and the quick view, "Full autonomy", a word plus an icon (never color alone); nothing for Asks first. Every change is an Activity row ("You gave ada Full autonomy"). Turning it on takes effect at the agent's next session; turning it off at its next start or wake, when processes are delivered (§3).
- **What it changes:** which of two sealed organization processes applies. Both ship with every new organization and only the owner can change them (decision 8):
  - **`asks-first`** (applies to agents at Asks first): before a non-routine call, ask your lead, or the owner at the top (a critical decision always goes to the owner, even when your lead is an agent), with the options and a recommendation, and keep working on everything else meanwhile.
  - **`decide-and-escalate`** (applies to agents at Full autonomy; ada's `docs/processes/decide-and-escalate.md`): decide routine and reversible calls yourself and write down what you decided and why; when unsure, consult a fresh peer, giving it the question and the facts, not your conclusion, until you agree; escalate only when there's no consensus (to your lead, or the owner at the top), when the decision is critical (always to the owner, even when your lead is an agent), or when only someone else can unblock you; keep working on everything else meanwhile.
- **A consult** is a subagent inside the agent's own run, so its cost counts toward that run (Insights) and its busy limit, with no separate run or hire. A runtime that can't start a subagent escalates instead of consulting.
- **Critical decisions** (Settings › Autonomy, the owner's): an editable list, by default **security, data loss, money or spend, irreversible or one-way, legal, product scope or priority**. Teams and projects can add items through their own processes; only the owner can remove one.
- **Autonomy changes when an agent asks, never what it may do.** Full autonomy doesn't let an agent approve hires (Can approve hires stays a separate flag, with Settings › Hiring's limits), approve its own changes, grant **Allow once…**, or pass a spending guardrail; spending stays on the critical list. Approvals other processes require (review READY, the lead's go, a process change's approval) still apply.
- **Escalations** reach the owner in **Needs you** (an agent lead, when it's the right person for a no-consensus question, gets it in its inbox, decision 11): "ada needs you: <question>", what it blocks, the options as buttons with the recommendation marked "Recommended", the peer's view ("The consult agreed" or "The consult disagreed: <why>") and **Reply…**. The answer goes back to the agent, like an Ask in Chat (`chat-ux.md` §2). States: waiting (with how long), answered ("Answered by you: <option>"), and withdrawn by the agent ("ada resolved it: <how>").
- **Decisions taken**, for auditing without noise: the main record is a **Decisions** section on the task (each: the decision, the options weighed, whether a peer was consulted and its view, and whether it can be undone). Each decision and consult is also one compact Activity row of kind **Decisions**, hidden from Home and from Activity's default view, shown with the Decisions filter. A decision with no current task goes on the agent's Activity. A lasting architectural decision becomes an ADR (`decisions-and-tech-debt-ux.md`), not a log entry.
- **For agents (CLI, proposed for ada, A73):** `org-agent decision record "<decision>" --option "…" --option "…" [--consulted "<the peer's view>"] [--reversible|--one-way] [--task <ref>]`, and `org-agent escalate "<question>" --option "…" --recommend "<option>" [--consulted "…"] [--blocks "<what>"]`.

## 5. UX acceptance criteria (PROC)

- PROC-1 · Selected process/standard content uses the envelope/path in §1, stable IDs, explicit scope, lifecycle and accountable owner. **Deferred resolver-key/approval extension:** key clashes, overrides/version history and change actions require a selected contract; current helper inputs are versioned separately.
- PROC-2 · **Deferred resolver:** candidate order is organization → accountable team ancestry → optional project; assignment/home-team/contributor records are not extra owner teams. Contributor/private/no-work participation needs an explicit decision.
- PROC-3 · Selected overrides default allowed unless an applicable ancestor explicitly prohibits them. **Deferred resolver UI:** explain the prohibiting source; a sibling conflict or project override cannot defeat that prohibition.
- PROC-4 · **Deferred process/delivery/approval engine:** *(after v1.0, F2-12 R3)* *(rev 4)* An overridable process can be turned off at a lower level with a reason, shown as "Turned off for <team or project name>: <reason>". For one-open-branch (once unsealed by the owner), a turning-off counts only when approved by someone other than its author; one that merged without that shows "On · turning it off needs approval by someone other than its author (<author>)" with **Ask for approval…** and **Have an agent draft it…**, each a new proposal through the change flow.
