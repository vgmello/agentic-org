## 1. The problem

**[F] The owner, via ada, 2026-11-12 (near-verbatim):** "A retrospective is something the owner schedules (every 1, 2, 4 hours, whatever), with instructions for what the retro is, and then they are done. It belongs to a team (or project): schedule + lead + team + instructions. When it fires the SYSTEM sends a system message to the lead ('trigger the retrospective'), and a message to every agent on the team ('we are starting the retro, led by X, finish the work in hand'), then the lead runs it. No agent tracks the schedule, no manual reminders, no status kept by hand."
**[F] Earlier:** agents raise retro items mid-turn with `retro add`; the system knows the agent's project and the last and next retro, so the agent never names a project; `retro list` shows everything raised; items close as they're resolved.

**[F] What exists:**

| Exists | Missing |
| --- | --- |
| An interim tool in the agents repo, `bin/retro`: per-agent event files, ids safe under concurrent adds, nearest-slot consumption, "unscheduled" fallback (F1-F7). | Its scheduler is documented but not wired, and the schedule has no zone, lead or instructions (F3, F8). Carry-over is only a label (F5). |
| ada runs real rhythms by hand: a 4-hourly checkpoint, a daily 18:00 retro, hourly reports, a numbered rules file (F9-F11). | Anything that fires them. Retro 1 was owner-triggered and stopped eight agents for ~40 min (F9). |
| Per-agent schedules as data (spec + IANA zone) and a run-modes design where a message wakes an office-run agent (F13, F14). | No code fires any schedule, and today a message wakes only an agent already listening (F12, F13). No "System" sender (F15). |
| Eden's `retrospectives-ux.md` rev 4: feature-or-interval retros with owner approval of action items (F18). This PRD supersedes it for v1.0 (D22). | A general primitive: the same need covers a team check-in, a machine-health check, an hourly estimate and a weekly audit (ada's inventory, §10). |

**[O] Why it matters.** An org of agents that never sleeps needs a rhythm nobody has to remember. Today the rhythm lives in ada's context, so it stops when she is busy, compacted or asleep, and every reminder costs a turn. The owner should set a routine once and only hear about it when it fails.

## 2. Users and jobs

- **The owner:** "Set the retro up once and forget it." "Tell me only if it stops happening." "What did the last one decide?"
- **A routine's lead (an agent):** "Tell me when it's time, with everything I need in one message." "Record the outcome once, in a structure others can read."
- **A participant (an agent):** "Tell me it's starting without interrupting my turn." "Let me raise an item in one call, without naming a project."

## 3. Goals, non-goals and success

**Goals:**
- A routine is set up once, in one form, and from then on fires, announces and records itself. No agent tracks a schedule; no person sends a reminder.
- Raising a retro item is one CLI call with no project argument, and it never fails because a schedule is broken.
- Every occurrence ends in one recorded state, and the owner hears about routines only when one misses twice or needs a lead.

**Non-goals (v1.0):**
- interrupting a busy agent (D7);
- routines created or edited by agents, or by people other than the owner (D11);
- people as leads (D3);
- automatic actions from a routine (health checks with approved actions are Next, §10);
- owner approval of a retro's action items, an evidence panel, or rules applied to every agent automatically (D22);
- feature-delivered triggers (§10);
- notifications outside the app.

**Success (D13), measured from release:**

| Metric | Target | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routines firing on time | ≥ 99% of due slots reach Announced or a recorded Skipped within 1 min of the slot | occurrence records vs. computed slots |
| Missed rate | ≤ 5% of Announced occurrences end Missed or Closed without outcome after 4 h (the auto-close); Closed without outcome "lead left" and Cancelled occurrences (including "lead left") don't count, consistent with D17 **[A]** | occurrence records |
| Announce → start | median ≤ 10 min **[A]** | Announced and Running times |
| Retro items closed within 2 occurrences | ≥ 80% **[A]** | item close vs. carried count |
| Owner touches for routine upkeep | 0 per week after setup | edits, run-nows, skips and cancels by the owner, excluding creation week |

## 5. The model

### 5.1 Occurrence transitions [O]

Anything not listed is refused. Only one open occurrence per routine, ever.

| From | To | Who / trigger | Condition | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| — | Scheduled | system | the routine is created, resumed or edited, or its previous slot was processed (fired, skipped or consumed) | the next slot after now that has no recorded occurrence (fired, skipped, or consumed by a run-now, §5.5). An enabled routine always has exactly one Scheduled occurrence, even while another is open, except when its next slot can't be computed (§5.8). A routine that needs a lead keeps its Scheduled occurrence, and items attach to it (§5.8), but it shows no next slot (§5.4, §7): no retro will run until it has a lead. |
| Scheduled | Announced | system, at the slot | routine enabled; it has a valid lead; no skip rule holds; no open occurrence | M1 to the lead, M2 to each participant, Activity line A1; grace starts at the slot (D7) |
| Scheduled | Skipped | system, at the slot | the lead has left the scope or was offboarded (D23) | reason "no lead"; A5; no messages; open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count; neutral for the missed-twice alert (the needs-a-lead Problem covers it, §5.4) |
| Scheduled | Skipped | system, at the slot | a skip rule holds (§5.4), or an occurrence is still open | Activity line A5 with the first reason that holds, by the §5.4 precedence; no messages; open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count (with an occurrence still open, they join it) |
| Scheduled | Skipped | system, when the server returns from an outage | one or more slots passed their grace while it was down (D18, §5.3); this takes precedence over every skip rule (§5.4) | **one** record for the outage, covering every slot lost in it, with one occurrence number; reason "server offline"; A5 with the slot count; no messages; open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count; neutral for the missed-twice alert |
| Scheduled | Skipped | owner or lead, `skip`, before the slot | — | reason "Skipped by {name}"; A5; open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count |
| Scheduled | Skipped | system, on a run-now | the run-now consumes this slot (§5.5) | reason "Ran early at {time}"; A5; all its items, closed ones included, move by the attachment rule (§5.8) to the run-now's open occurrence, no carry count; the closed ones count in that occurrence's {r} and {c} (as for a withdrawn occurrence, §4) |
| Scheduled | (withdrawn, no record) | system | routine paused, deleted, or its schedule, zone or kind edited | a new Scheduled is computed on resume or edit; on an edit, the withdrawn occurrence's items, closed ones included, move by the attachment rule (§5.8) to the newly computed occurrence, except that an edit changing a Retro routine's kind to another kind makes them Unscheduled in the scope (§5.8); on a pause, they all, closed ones included, wait for Resume and then move the same way to the occurrence computed on Resume, where the closed ones count in its {r} and {c} (§5.6); on a delete, §5.8's deleted rule applies |
| — | Announced (ad hoc) | owner or lead, `run-now` | routine enabled; it has a valid lead; no open occurrence | as Scheduled → Announced; grace starts at the run-now |
| Announced | Running | the occurrence's announced lead only | `routines start` (§7.3), or the announced lead's first action on it: a `retro close` of one of its items, or a `retro reopen` of an item that then attaches to it (the attachment rule, §5.8). A close or reopen of one of its items by anyone else allowed (§5.2: the routine's current lead after an edit, the scope manager, the owner, the item's author) closes or reopens the item and doesn't start the occurrence | A2 |
| Announced | Closed | the lead | `routines close` with an outcome | recorded as started and closed at once; M3; A3; carry-over (§5.8, by the attachment rule) |
| Announced | Missed | system | the deadline (slot + grace, §4) passed without a start | A6; counts toward the alert (§5.4); open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count |
| Announced | Cancelled | owner, `cancel` | — | M4 to the lead and participants; A7; open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count |
| Running | Closed | the lead | `routines close` with an outcome | M3; A3; carry-over |
| Running | Closed without outcome | system | 4 h after Running began, no close | M3 (variant); A4; carry-over; counts as a miss for the alert |
| Running | Cancelled | — | refused: "{routine} has started: its lead closes it." | — |
| Announced | Cancelled | system | the occurrence's lead left the scope or was offboarded (D23) | reason "lead left"; M4 (lead-left variant) to the participants; A7 (variant); open items move by the attachment rule (§5.8), no carry count; neutral for the missed-twice count |
| Running | Closed without outcome | system | the occurrence's lead left the scope or was offboarded (D23) | reason "lead left"; M3 (lead-left variant) to the participants; A4 (variant); carry-over; **neutral** for the missed-twice count (the needs-a-lead Problem covers it, §5.4) |

Terminal states: Closed, Closed without outcome, Skipped, Missed, Cancelled. A paused routine keeps an open occurrence: its lead can still start and close it **[O]**. An open occurrence keeps everything it was announced with (lead, participants, instructions, grace), whatever edits the routine gets meanwhile; the only exception is the lead-left rule above, which ends it **[O]**. Where an item attaches (added, reopened or moved) is the attachment rule of §5.8.

### 5.3 Schedule and time (D2)

| Schedule | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **Every N** | Every N minutes, hours or days, N ≥ 15 min, ≤ 7 days. Slots are real elapsed time from an **anchor** (D15), so DST doesn't move them: the slots are anchor + k×N (k = 0, 1, 2, …), and the first slot is the first of them strictly after now. The anchor is the first candidate slot: it is used only if it is strictly after now, else the next anchor + k×N that is. The owner sets it with "Starting at" in the form (§8) or `--from HH:MM` (indicative, §7.1; HH:MM in the routine's zone on the day of the create or edit). Default: the creation time rounded up to the next quarter hour; a creation exactly on a quarter hour uses that time (so created at 09:00, every 1 hour: anchor 09:00, which is not after now, so the first slot is 10:00; created at 09:07: anchor and first slot 09:15). An edit with `--from` sets a new anchor; an edit that switches a routine to Every N without `--from` takes the edit time rounded the same way; any other edit keeps the anchor. |
| **At a time on chosen days** | HH:MM local time on any set of weekdays ("daily" = all seven; "weekly" = one). Local wall time. |
| **Advanced (cron)** | 5-field cron, minute resolution, local wall time. Refused if any two slots in the next 30 days are less than 15 min apart. |

- **Zone:** an IANA zone, default the creating account's zone (away-digest Z1). Slots and deadlines show in this zone with their date and offset, "Thu 12 Nov, 18:00 Lisbon (UTC+0)", never "Today" or "tomorrow"; every other UI time follows the viewer's profile zone (§4 time format, an extension of Z2 for schedules).
- **DST (wall-time kinds):** a 09:00 routine fires at 09:00 local on both sides of a change. A local time that doesn't exist (spring forward) fires once, at the first minute after the gap; a local time that happens twice (fall back) fires once, at its first instance.
- **Server down at a slot (D18):** on return, a slot still within its grace fires once, late, labelled "Fired at Thu 12 Nov, 09:12 Lisbon (UTC+0) instead of Thu 12 Nov, 09:00 Lisbon (UTC+0) (the server was offline)"; the label shows on the occurrence's history row (§8) and in its A1 line (§5.6). Grace still runs from the slot (§4), so the late M1 gives the original deadline. Slots past their grace are recorded as **one** Skipped "server offline" record per outage, covering every slot lost in it and taking one occurrence number (§4), with no messages and no count toward the alert; its A5 line gives the count ("server offline (3 slots)"). "Server offline" takes precedence over every skip rule (§5.4): a lost slot is recorded "server offline" even when a skip rule would also have held.

### 5.4 Skip rules, misses and the alert (D5)

| Rule | Offered for | Holds when (checked at the slot) | Default |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No valid lead (D23) | all, always | the lead has left the scope or was offboarded | always |
| Skip if the lead is paused | all | the lead's status is Paused (owner pause, or an agent budget pause, costs req 20) | on |
| Previous still open | all, always | an occurrence of this routine is Announced or Running | always |
| Skip if there are no open items | Retro | no open item is attached to the occurrence, and its scope has no open Unscheduled item (a scope's Unscheduled items count as open items of each of its Retro routines, §5.8) | on |

- **Precedence:** when several rules hold at one slot, the first in this order wins and is the reason A5 records: no lead > lead paused > previous occurrence still open > no open items. A slot lost in an outage (past its grace when the server returns, §5.3) is recorded "server offline" whatever else holds: it takes precedence over every rule above.
- **Missed:** Announced with no start by the deadline (slot + grace, §4). **Closed without outcome** after 4 h (§5.1) counts as a miss for the alert. Skipped "no lead", Cancelled "lead left" and Closed without outcome "lead left" don't: the needs-a-lead Problem covers them (D23).
- **Needs a lead (D23):** as soon as a routine has no valid lead (its lead left the scope or was offboarded), the routine shows "Needs a lead" and the system opens **one** Inbox **Problem** for the owner, "Routine {name} needs a lead". It **can't be dismissed**. It clears (Handled) when the owner sets a valid lead (edit), pauses or deletes the routine, or when the assigned lead rejoins the scope (the routine is valid again with no edit). A **paused** routine whose lead leaves opens no needs-a-lead Problem while it stays paused; it opens on Resume if the routine still has no valid lead. Run now and skip are refused meanwhile, and the routine shows no next slot (§7: "Needs a lead" in its place), though its Scheduled occurrence exists and items attach to it (§5.1, §5.8): no retro will run until it has a lead. `retro add` still works. If the lead leaves while an occurrence is open, that occurrence ends at once by the lead-left rows of §5.1 (Announced → Cancelled "lead left"; Running → Closed without outcome "lead left").
- **The alert:** two consecutive misses of one routine open **one** Inbox **Problem**, "Routine {name} missed twice". Skipped occurrences (including "no lead"), Cancelled ones (including "lead left") and Closed without outcome "lead left" are neutral: they neither count nor break the run of misses (D17). It clears automatically, and the consecutive-miss count resets, when an occurrence of that routine is Closed (with an outcome), or the owner pauses, edits or deletes the routine. A paused routine's open occurrence that ends Missed or Closed without outcome counts toward the alert like any other (the pause reset the count, so it counts from zero). It **can't be dismissed**: only run error and crash items can be (IA D26, the lens rule).
- **A routine itself creates exactly two kinds of Inbox item** (both Problems, both non-dismissible: "missed twice" and "needs a lead"). Action items are tasks, and their Inbox items follow `tasks-v1` (an action assigned to the owner opens a Hand-off, `tasks-v1` §4.4, AC-TK6-6).

### 5.5 Run now and slot consumption (D4)

- `run-now` creates an ad-hoc occurrence, Announced at once, with the same messages and grace.
- It **consumes the nearest slot that hasn't fired yet** if one lies within half the interval (Every N), or on the same local day (time-on-days and cron). A tie goes to the earlier slot. The consumed slot is recorded Skipped "Ran early at {time}".
- Refused while an occurrence is open, while the routine is paused, or while it needs a lead (§7.3).

### 5.6 Messages and Activity (D6)

All messages are from **System**, delivered so they wake the recipient (requirement 9). Busy recipients get them at the end of their current turn (D7). A paused recipient (owner pause, or a budget pause) is not woken: the message is queued for when it runs again (costs req 20); when attendance is recorded (§5.7, D21), a paused participant counts absent unless it reads M2 before the close. Messages go to agents only; no message is created for a person (D3). Item text inside a message is quoted and labelled untrusted. Messages are agent-facing: every time in them prints in the schedule's zone with its date and offset (§4 time format).

| M2 | each participant (agents) | Announced | `{routine} is starting` |

| M4 | the lead and each participant (lead-left variant: the participants only) | Cancelled (D16) | `{routine} cancelled` |

Where " · " separates fields on one line, it is literal. Inside a block every character is delivered as shown; elsewhere in this PRD, `\"` inside a quoted text means a literal ". **A close is all or nothing, for every input [O].** The whole close is validated before anything is applied. If any input is invalid, the close is refused with the §7.3 text for the first invalid input and nothing is applied: the occurrence stays as it was, no item is closed or moved, and no task is created. Invalid inputs: a missing summary; an `--item` not attached to the occurrence ("RI-{n} isn't part of {RT-n#m}.", checked first) or attached but already closed ("RI-{n} is already closed."); a `work:` naming a task or bug that doesn't exist or the lead can't read ("No task or bug {key} that you can read."); a `--follow-up` who isn't a member of the scope and isn't the owner ("{name} isn't a member of {scope}."); an action with an invalid project or assignee (above, §7.3). **When several inputs are invalid**, the refusal is for the first in this order: the missing summary, then `--item`, then `work:`, then `--follow-up`, then action items (each action's `--project`, then its `--assignee`, in the order the actions are given).

### 5.8 Retro items (D9)

**The attachment rule (one rule, used everywhere) [O]:** any item that has a target routine (added, reopened, moved from Unscheduled or from a deleted routine, carried over from a Closed, Closed without outcome (including the 4 h auto-close), Skipped, Missed or Cancelled occurrence, or on a Scheduled occurrence withdrawn by an edit or consumed by a run-now) attaches to that routine's **open** occurrence (Announced or Running) if there is one, else to its next Scheduled occurrence. A paused routine has no Scheduled occurrence: an item sent to it waits and attaches by this rule on Resume. A routine that needs a lead keeps computing its slots (each is Skipped "no lead", §5.1): an item sent to it attaches to its next Scheduled occurrence and, as each slot is Skipped "no lead", carries over to the next occurrence (no carry count, as for any Skipped), until a lead is set (AC-CER12-4). Every other section refers to this rule.

**Lifecycle:** Open ⇄ Closed (close needs a note; reopen returns it to Open, with the routine it was last attached to as its target, by the attachment rule; if that routine is deleted or paused, scope resolution below applies, as for an add by the item's author). A closed item stays with the occurrence where it closed, except when that occurrence is withdrawn (an edit or a pause, §5.1): then it moves with the occurrence's open items, by the schedule-change and paused rules below, and counts as closed in the outcome of the occurrence it lands on ({r} and {c}, §5.6). An Unscheduled item is closed or reopened by its author, the scope manager or the owner (§5.2); a reopened Unscheduled item stays Unscheduled in its scope. The `retro add` and `retro reopen` outputs are exact (§7.2).

**Scope resolution for `retro add`** (in order; first match wins). `retro next` uses the same resolution (§7.2). **Current task** is defined in §4 (`costs-and-budgets-v1.md` §4); with no current task, step 1 doesn't match and the team step (2), then the staffed-project step (3), apply. Step 0 comes before the current task: an agent taking part in an open retro raises items into it, so M2's "raise anything for it with `org-agent retro add`" holds.

| # | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | The agent is a participant (§4) or the announced lead of an open occurrence (Announced or Running) of a Retro routine | that occurrence (its routine is the target, and the item attaches to the open occurrence by the attachment rule); with several, the most recently announced |
| 1 | The agent has a current task, and that task's project has a Retro routine | that project |
| 2 | Otherwise, a team the agent is a member of has a Retro routine | that team |
| 3 | Otherwise, a project staffed by one of the agent's teams has a Retro routine | that project |
| 4 | Several routines match at step 1, 2 or 3 | the one whose next slot is earliest; enabled routines rank before paused ones, and routines with no next slot (paused, or needing a lead) rank after those with one, then by lower `RT-n` (D20) |
| 5 | None match (steps 0-3) | recorded **Unscheduled** in the current task's project, else the team of the agent's oldest membership (D20); it attaches by the Unscheduled rule below. Only here is an item Unscheduled by scope resolution |

- **The Unscheduled rule [O]:** an Unscheduled item's target is the enabled Retro routine in its scope with a valid lead whose next slot is earliest (a tie goes to the lower `RT-n`); it attaches to that routine by the attachment rule, as soon as such a routine exists: at once if there is one when the item becomes Unscheduled, else when a Retro routine is created there, resumed, given a valid lead, or its next slot can be computed again. A paused routine, or one that needs a lead, shows no next slot (§7) and is never an Unscheduled item's target. Until it attaches, the item counts as an open item of each of the scope's Retro routines for "Skip if there are no open items" (§5.4).
- **"Raised during the retro" [O]:** an item that first attached to an occurrence while that occurrence was Running (added, reopened or moved into it by the attachment rule) carries the label "raised during the retro" on that occurrence. It shows on the `retro list` item line (" · raised during the retro" appended, §7.2), on the item lines of `routines show RT-n#m` (§7.1), and on the occurrence page (§8); in `--json` each item has the boolean `raised_during` (an indicative field name: Rowan owns names, as for flags, §7), true exactly when the item first attached to that occurrence while it was Running. An item attached while the occurrence was Scheduled or Announced doesn't carry it. The label belongs to that occurrence: an item carried over to the next occurrence doesn't carry it there (`raised_during` false), unless it first attaches to that one while it is Running.
- **Schedule changes:** when an edit to the schedule, zone or kind withdraws the Scheduled occurrence an item is attached to, the item (closed ones included) moves to the newly computed occurrence by the attachment rule (the routine's open occurrence first, if there is one), with its count unchanged. **Kind changed away from Retro [O]:** when an edit changes a Retro routine's kind to another kind, the items of its withdrawn Scheduled occurrence, closed ones included, become Unscheduled in the routine's scope, with their counts unchanged, and attach by the Unscheduled rule (to another enabled Retro routine there with a valid lead, if any). An occurrence open at that edit keeps its items (it keeps what it was announced with, §5.1); when it ends, its open items become Unscheduled the same way instead of carrying over. A run-now's consumed slot moves its items to the run-now's open occurrence the same way.
- **Carry-over:** when an occurrence ends Closed or Closed without outcome, each item attached to it and still open moves by the attachment rule (the routine's open occurrence, else its next Scheduled occurrence) and its count rises by one ("carried over ×N"). Missed, Skipped (including "no lead") and Cancelled move open items by the same rule without counting; a slot Skipped while another occurrence is still open moves its items to that open occurrence. On a paused routine, items wait and attach by the attachment rule after Resume. **Pause [O]:** a pause withdraws the Scheduled occurrence (§5.1); all its items, closed ones included, wait for Resume (`retro list` shows them under "Waiting for resume", §7.2) and then move to the occurrence computed on Resume, where the closed ones count in its {r} and {c} (§5.6).
- **Deleted routine:** its open items become Unscheduled in that scope and attach by the Unscheduled rule (so by the attachment rule) as soon as a target exists there.
- **Never fails on the schedule:** a paused, deleted or unreadable routine, one that needs a lead, or none, never refuses an add; the item is recorded and the output says where (§7.2). A paused routine, or one that needs a lead, takes an item that scope resolution sends to it, by the attachment rule (on a paused routine it waits for Resume; on one that needs a lead it carries over at each "no lead" skip); only Unscheduled items skip it (the Unscheduled rule). A routine whose next slot can't be computed can't take it: the item is recorded Unscheduled in that routine's scope and attaches by the Unscheduled rule.
- **Identity** comes from the authenticated connection only, never an argument or the environment.
- **Text:** plain, 1-2000 characters after trimming, counted as Unicode code points; control, format (zero-width, bidi) and line-separator characters are refused at input. Shown as plain text to every viewer, in every surface (UI, CLI, messages): no markup, links not clickable, agent emoji stripped at render (IA D7). `--ref` must name a task, bug or message the author can read; it shows as a link only to viewers who can read its target, else as plain text.
- **Ordering** follows the order the server records them, never client or wall-clock time.

## 6. Requirements for engineering (Rowan designs the how)

1. **Routines** as org data with the §4 fields, scoped to one team or project, with the §5.2 permissions enforced on the server: in v1.0 only the owner configures, and the lead must be an agent member of the scope (D3, D11).
2. **A scheduler** that computes slots per §5.3 (interval elapsed time; wall-time kinds in the routine's zone, DST rules as stated) and fires each slot **exactly once**: a retried fire, a restart or two attempts to fire the same slot never create a duplicate occurrence, two open occurrences or a duplicate message.
3. **The occurrence state machine** of §5.1, enforced on the server, with the timers for grace and the 4 h auto-close.
4. **Messages M1-M4** from a System sender that no member can impersonate or reply-spoof, sent at most once per (occurrence, recipient, kind). How the sender is represented is R1.
5. **Activity lines A1-A7** and the two Inbox Problems (§5.4), "missed twice" and "needs a lead", mapped into D26's Problems kind with their clearing rules (and the miss count reset); neither is dismissible.
6. **The outcome record** (§5.7) stored with the occurrence; action items created through the tasks API with the lead as creator and Asked by, with no approval step (D22), so their Inbox items are the tasks API's (an action assigned to the owner opens a Hand-off, `tasks-v1` §4.4). A close is validated whole before anything is applied: one invalid input refuses it all (§5.7).
7. **Retro items** (§5.8): server-assigned `RI-n` ids, unique under concurrent adds; item ids are never reused; scope resolution; carry-over at close; sanitization at input; ordering in the order the server records them.
8. **Participants and lead resolved at fire time** from current memberships (F16); the occurrence keeps the values it was announced with. A lead who has left the scope or was offboarded makes the routine "Needs a lead", opens the needs-a-lead Problem (on Resume, for a paused routine), and makes each slot Skipped "no lead" (D23). An open occurrence whose announced lead leaves ends at once: Announced → Cancelled "lead left", Running → Closed without outcome "lead left" (§5.1).
9. **Prerequisite (P0, ada's inventory item 2): message delivery that wakes the recipient.** A routine message to an idle or asleep agent must start a turn; to a busy one, it must be delivered at the end of the current turn (D7); to a paused one, it is queued and doesn't wake it (costs req 20). Today only a live listener is woken (F12) and the run-modes design that wakes office-run agents is not built (F13). Routines can't ship without it.
10. **`org-agent routines` and `org-agent retro`** (§7), compact output, `--json` everywhere; a retried write (a fire, an add, a close or any other) never creates a duplicate. `org-agent ceremonies` is an alias of `routines` with identical behaviour (D24).
11. **Migration:** the interim tool's open items (agents repo) can be imported once into the matching scope as Unscheduled items, attributed to their authors (F2) **[A]** ada wants this; confirm.
